# Rubric: steps 10 to 12 of the churn case

The test checks your numbers (steps 7 to 9). This rubric checks what you do with them. Score each row 0, 1 or 2. A total of 5 or 6 is ready to send to Neha; 3 or 4 needs one more pass; below 3, start again from your step 9.

| Step | 0 points | 1 point | 2 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10. Recommendation is specific | "Improve customer experience" or "reduce churn across the board". No programme, owner or time | Names the programme (onboarding calls) but not who gets it, who runs it or when, or funds both programmes | One programme with an owner, a trigger and a start: customer success calls new customers with no bank feed by day 7, from next month, randomised for the first months; the small-business plan waits |

## Checks before you send

- Every churn rate you quote is per customer-month or from the life table, never churned / customers across different signup dates.
- You compared programmes on MRR kept, and said why counting customers points the other way.
- The impact is labelled as an upper bound or carries its assumption.

## A two-point answer, in short

"Fund onboarding calls: from next month customer success calls every new customer who has not connected a bank feed by day 7, teams first, offering the call to a random half for the first two months. If a call closes the whole gap to onboarded customers it keeps about Rs 23,200 of MRR a month; assuming it closes half, about Rs 11,600 a month, against at most Rs 7,300 for a small-business plan. Caveats: onboarded customers may just be more committed, which the randomised months will show; annual customers need a renewal programme at month 11 instead."
